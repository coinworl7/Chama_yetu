# Treasurer interview script

**Time:** 20 minutes. **Goal:** hear the current process and the last time it failed. Not to pitch.

## Rules before you start

- Say clearly: *"This is a project about record-keeping, not a sales call. Nothing you say gets
  attached to your group's name."*
- Ask about **the last month**, not about chamas in general. Specifics beat opinions.
- When they say a problem, ask **"what did you do about it?"** — the workaround tells you what to build.
- Never say "we could build an app for that." The moment you pitch, they start being polite and you
  stop learning.
- If they offer to show you their Excel or M-Pesa statement, say yes and take notes on the columns.

## The 10 questions

**1. Walk me from the day contributions are due to the day you know who has paid.**
> Probes: What's the first thing you do? Where do you write it? How long does that take end to end?

**2. What do you record it in today?**
> Probes: Show me if you can. Who else can open that file? What happens if your phone is lost?

**3. How long does the monthly update take you, honestly?**
> Probes: Which part takes the longest? Have you ever skipped a month?

**4. Tell me about the last time the records caused a problem.**
> Probes: What happened? How did you find out? Who was involved?

**5. How was it resolved?**
> Probes: How long did it take? Did anyone leave the group? Did you have to call a meeting?

**6. How does a member check their own balance today?**
> Probes: Do they ask you? How often? What do you send them — screenshot, message, nothing?

**7. What about loans, interest and fines — how do you track those?**
> Probes: Is there a written rule? Who calculates the interest? Has a calculation been disputed?

**8. If a member asked "how much is in the kitty right now", how fast could you answer?**
> Probes: Would you be confident in the number? Would you have to check two places?

**9. What have you tried before — an app, a book, a different sheet?**
> Probes: Why did you stop? What was the thing that made you give up?

**10. What would you pay to never have that argument again?**
> Probes: Who decides — you, the chair, or the whole group? Is there a budget for group expenses?

## Closing (2 minutes)

> "That's genuinely useful. I'm building a one-page monthly statement — every member's contribution,
> arrears, loan balances, and a closing balance that matches your M-Pesa. If I made one for your last
> month, would you look at it? I'd do the first one free."

Then ask, whatever the answer:

> "Who else in your network keeps these records? I'd like to talk to two more treasurers."

## Scoring — score each interview 0–3 on each row

| Signal | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | "It's fine" | mild annoyance | a real monthly headache | lost money or a member over it |
| **Workaround cost** | none | minutes | hours per month | they avoid it, or it's wrong |
| **Transparency gap** | members see everything | ask and get answers | only the treasurer knows | members suspect the books |
| **Willingness to pay** | no | "maybe if free" | "depends on price" | named a number unprompted |
| **Decision power** | not involved | influences | shares decision | decides alone |

**9+ = hot.** Ask for records the same week. **5–8 = warm**, follow up after the first statement exists.
**4 or less = wrong segment**, note it and move on.

## What to record after each call

One paragraph in `notes/` — the exact phrase they used for the problem, the current tool, the number
of members, the monthly contribution, and whether they named a price. Their words become your sales
copy later; your paraphrase will not.
